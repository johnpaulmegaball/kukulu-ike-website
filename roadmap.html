Roadmap

Your Path From Seed to Scientist

A developmental pathway showing how STEM curiosity grows into capability and purpose, from a student’s earliest exploration through their final year of high school.

Each stage builds on the one before it, from early curiosity to career readiness. Select a stage below to see what it involves.

  • Students build early curiosity through guided exploration and hands-on discovery of science and technology.
  • Engage with books, museum visits, and simple experiments that make science feel approachable.
  • Explore introductory concepts in building, design, and coding through age-appropriate kits and tools such as Scratch Jr.
  • Participate in school science fairs or STEM enrichment days that introduce new fields of interest.
  • Students develop foundational STEM skills while exploring a range of scientific and technical disciplines.
  • Join school-based STEM programs such as a science club, math team, or robotics team.
  • Gain first exposure to structured competition through programs like MATHCOUNTS or Science Olympiad Division B.
  • Build early technical literacy through introductory coding platforms such as Scratch or Code.org.
  • Students strengthen core STEM skills and begin connecting classroom learning to real-world applications.
  • Pursue rigorous coursework in mathematics and science to build a strong academic foundation.
  • Compete in established programs such as Science Olympiad, FIRST LEGO League, or a regional math league.
  • Research high school STEM programs and plan a course schedule aligned with emerging interests.
  • Students establish a strong foundation in high school STEM through structured coursework and early involvement.
  • Join a STEM-focused club or team to build technical experience and community connections.
  • Explore beginner-friendly opportunities through the Opportunity Explorer, such as FIRST Tech Challenge or Science Olympiad.
  • Start keeping your own record of volunteer hours and activities as part of a growing STEM profile.
  • Students pursue deeper exploration of specific STEM interests through hands-on projects and applied learning.
  • Take on leadership responsibilities within a club, team, or ongoing project.
  • Gain early research or internship exposure through programs such as the PISCES STEM Program.
  • Connect with mentors to better understand professional STEM pathways and career expectations.
  • Students undertake advanced, self-directed projects that reflect growing technical skill and independence.
  • Compete at a higher level through regional or state competitions and science fairs.
  • Engage in independent research, such as through a structured program like Polygence.
  • Begin researching postsecondary programs and institutions aligned with academic and career goals.
  • Students apply accumulated knowledge and experience to prepare for postsecondary STEM education or careers.
  • Represent their STEM growth through college applications, portfolios, and personal essays.
  • Mentor younger students, extending the Kūkulu ʻIke cycle of learning to the next generation.
  • Finalize community service records and reflect on the progression from early curiosity to career readiness.
